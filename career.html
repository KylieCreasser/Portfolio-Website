Ongoing content work

Career Center Content

This role gave me the chance to create reels, student stories, event content, and campus graphics—and then look at the numbers to see what people responded to.

My role
Marketing & content intern
Cadence
1–2 reels per week
Channels
Instagram, LinkedIn, blog, LCD
Focus
Awareness & engagement

01

The job

The Career Center always had something worth sharing: events, resources, employer partnerships, and student stories. The challenge was making students notice information that could easily feel like another campus announcement.

I created short-form video, student features, blog graphics, and digital signage while adjusting to what was most important on campus each week.

02

How I approached it

I didn’t want every post to look or sound the same. Resource content needed an easy hook, event content needed a reason to act now, and student stories needed enough space to feel personal.

ATTENTION

Find the right hook

Use formats and trends that fit the message instead of forcing every idea into the same template.

CLARITY

Get to the point

Show students what the resource or event offered before asking them to do anything.

PERFORMANCE

Keep track of patterns

Compare reach, likes, theme, timing, and format across stronger and weaker posts.

03

Short-form video

I used trends when they made sense, but each reel still needed to leave students with something useful: a resource to check out, a partnership to know about, or an event to prepare for.

What stood out

The Clothing Closet reel had the strongest reach and likes of these three. It was a useful student resource packaged in a fun, easy-to-watch format—something I’d keep in mind when planning future content.

04

Student stories

I created a format that could work as both a longer blog story and a social post. That way, the same student feature could reach people in different places without feeling copied and pasted.

05

Campus screen graphics

Students might only see a campus screen for a few seconds while walking by. I kept the headline clear, made the resource obvious, and cut anything that didn’t need to be there.

06

Using the numbers

I tracked performance in Google Sheets so I could compare topic, timing, format, reach, and likes. I wanted the next idea to be based on what I was seeing, not just what I personally liked best.

What I’d add next

I’d include follower count, impressions, and engagement rate, then compare each post with the account’s typical performance. That would give the numbers more context.
